# The Six-Step Reasoning Chain: From the Atomic Level to Nucleated Self-Excitation

> Verbatim §2 of *Nucleated Self-Excitation: Conformational Propagation and Self-Sustained Excitation in Proteins, and a Universal Mechanism* (EN v1.2).
> Paper: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23860530 (version) · doi:10.5281/zenodo.23858233 (concept, all versions) · License CC-BY-4.0.

Each step below is established physical chemistry or a direct consequence thereof. **Nucleated self-excitation is not induced from biological phenomena; it grows out of the atomic level.**

**Step 1 (conformational states).** A protein possesses multiple local minima on its conformational energy landscape; each minimum corresponds to a (meta)stable conformational state, and inter-state conversion requires crossing an activation barrier. This is the standard picture of energy-landscape theory.

**Step 2 (the duality of the excited state).** Consider a molecule in a high-energy conformational state (e.g., a β-rich misfolded state). It is simultaneously two things:

- an **attenuator**: the high-energy state tends to return to lower energy, and cellular degradation channels (proteasome, autophagy) further remove it—spontaneous departure rate γ;
- an **exciter**: it can, through physical contact (a templating interface), induce a soluble molecule to convert into the same high-energy conformational state—excitation-transfer rate β.

**The excited state is at once a loss source and a gain source.** This is the atomic definition of *self-excitation*: the excitation of the system is carried by the already-excited.

**Step 3 (competition of two rates).** Define the rate ratio R = β/γ: the expected number of new molecules excited per excited molecule during its lifetime, against its expected dissipation.

**Step 4 (crossing one).** For R<1 the excitation chain decays (each generation shrinks; fluctuation). R=1 is critical. **For R>1 the excitation propagates self-sustainingly**—each excited molecule on average excites more than one successor; the gain chain expands exponentially. The lag phase is the accumulation period before R crosses one; the seed-dose effect is the forward shift of the crossing time by the number of injected excitations.

**Step 5 (population coherence → macroscopic nucleation).** Molecular-scale excitation-transfer events accumulate into macroscopic ordered structure (fibrils, phase-separated condensates): the system converts from molecular fluctuation (short residence) to macroscopic structure (long residence)—**the nucleation divide**. We note the boundary honestly: R>1 is a *necessary* condition for this conversion, not a sufficient one. Spatial coherence into a supracritical nucleus additionally requires the classical-nucleation conditions (surface-energy barrier, critical nucleus size, competition among nuclei—Ostwald regimes); the rate-ratio criterion governs whether excitation is self-sustaining, not whether a given fluctuation clears the barrier. The full condition is layered: R>1 (this paper) plus barrier crossing (classical nucleation theory).

**Step 6 (the cross-domain bridge).** This structure—medium units excited, exciting neighbors, gain against decay, collective mode established—does not depend on the specifics of protein chemistry. It is generalized in §5 of the paper to ecology, epidemiology and phase separation; the corresponding structure for physical waves is treated in a companion paper (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23858235, concept).
